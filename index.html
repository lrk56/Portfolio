About Me

My name is Lillian Kager, and I am a member of the class of 2029 at Drexel University. I am majoring in Computer Science, and minoring in Fine Arts and Data Science.

I am passionate about exploring the intersection of the arts and sciences, and how creativity and technical thinking can work together to solve problems, communicate ideas, and create meaningful experiences. I believe that art and science are not opposing disciplines, but complementary ways of understanding and interacting with the world. Through my work, I hope to explore how their combination can lead to new perspectives, thoughtful design, and innovative solutions.

My Process

Improvisation and a little mess are built into how I make things. I plan, but I do not expect the plan to survive contact with the work. A smudge, a bad sketch, or a bug that I did not see coming usually points me toward something better than what I started with.

I enjoy the slow middle of a project: trying something, stepping back, talking it through with others, and trying again. Whether I am layering an oil painting, building a sculpture, or debugging code, I would rather work through each stage properly than rush to a finished result. The finished piece tends to be stronger, and I understand it better because of the detours.

The same habits carry across everything I do. Communicating clearly, staying flexible, and being willing to start over are as much a part of my process as any technique.

Contact Me

I am always looking for new opportunities, collaborations, and connections, especially those that bring together art and technology. Whether you have a project in mind, a question about my work, or just want to talk about creativity and code, I would love to hear from you.

Title: CodeU

Type: Web application

Year: 2026

Built with: HTML, TailWind CSS, JavaScript, React JS, MongoDB

Description: CodeU is a programming learning platform built around learning by doing. Users work through hands-on coding challenges, try out code in a built-in online editor with instant feedback, and earn certificates for completing courses. Each learner gets a profile to track their progress. The site is organized around development roadmaps, such as front-end, back-end, full-stack, and mobile. Python is the first language available, with more planned for the future.

Title: Virtual Pet: Moose the Dog

Type: Interactive Web Project (CS 164)

Year: 2025

Built with: Tranquility, HTML, CSS

Description: An interactive virtual pet that models a finite state machine. Moose is based on a Miniature Australian Shepherd, and her eager, impatient personality drives the design. She moves between nine states, including neutral, hungry, happy, bored, sick, tired, frustrated, sleeping, and excited, depending on which of seven actions you choose (play, feed, pet, walk, nap, medicate, wake up). Timers add to the challenge: she gets bored if ignored for 25 seconds, hungry after 30, and falls asleep on her own if she's tired for too long. Feeding her when she isn't hungry makes her sick. I drew the pixel art for each state myself, inspired by the dog design in Stardew Valley.
